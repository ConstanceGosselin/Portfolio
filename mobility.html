DISASTER RISK · HUMAN MOBILITY

Disaster-Related
Population Mobility

Türkiye · 2023 earthquake · Master thesis

Analysis and validation of Meta movement data to investigate changes in population mobility following the 2023 Türkiye earthquake.

PROJECT OVERVIEW

Understanding population mobility after a major earthquake

Disasters can rapidly disrupt where and how people move. Understanding these changes can provide complementary information on the spatial and societal impacts of extreme events.

This project investigated the potential of Meta Movement Data to characterise changes in population mobility following the 6 February 2023 Türkiye earthquake.

The work combined data validation with a case study of earthquake-related mobility changes, using spatial and statistical approaches to examine how movement patterns changed following the event.

RESEARCH QUESTION

Can digital mobility data help reveal disaster-related population displacement?

How did population mobility change following the 2023 Türkiye earthquake, and to what extent can Meta Movement Data be used to investigate these changes?

DATA

Combining mobility and geographic data

01

Meta Movement Data

Aggregated and privacy-preserving movement data describing population mobility between geographic areas.

02

Geographic data

Administrative boundaries were used to organise, map and analyse mobility patterns across Türkiye.

03

Earthquake context

The analysis focused on the spatial and temporal context of the 6 February 2023 earthquake and its aftermath.

APPROACH

From data validation to disaster analysis

01

Data validation

Assessment of the behaviour and reliability of Meta Movement Data before using it to study disaster-related mobility changes.

02

Baseline mobility

Examination of mobility patterns before the earthquake to establish a reference for subsequent changes.

03

Post-earthquake changes

Comparison of mobility patterns following the earthquake to identify spatial and temporal changes in population movement.

04

Spatial and statistical analysis

Spatial and statistical methods were used to investigate differences between areas and identify patterns in the observed mobility response.

RESULTS

What the mobility data revealed

01

Mobility disruption after the earthquake

The earthquake was associated with substantial changes in population mobility, with the strongest responses occurring in areas directly affected by the disaster.

02

Spatial heterogeneity

Mobility responses were not spatially uniform. Differences between areas highlight the importance of considering the geographic context when interpreting digital mobility data after disasters.

DATA LIMITATIONS

Mobility data requires careful interpretation

Digital mobility data can provide valuable information during disasters, but it does not represent population movement perfectly.

The analysis highlighted the importance of considering spatial coverage, population characteristics and potential biases when interpreting mobility indicators.

These limitations are particularly important when mobility data are used to compare different places or population groups.

KEY TAKEAWAY

Digital mobility data can provide a complementary perspective on disaster impacts.

This project demonstrated the potential of Meta Movement Data for investigating population mobility during disaster situations, while highlighting the need for careful validation and interpretation of the underlying data.
