Climate & Tick Distribution in Europe

Investigating whether changes in relative humidity can help explain the emergence of tick species in new European regions.

Location Europe Period 2003–2023 Methods GIS · R · Spatial statistics
01

Research question

Tick species are influenced by environmental conditions that affect their activity and survival. As climate conditions change across Europe, changes in the distribution of vector species may have implications for public health.

This project investigates whether spatial and temporal variations in relative humidity are associated with the appearance of five tick species in new European regions between 2017 and 2023.

Research question

Does variation in relative humidity help explain the emergence of tick species in new European regions?

04

Climate patterns

Between 2017 and 2023, mean relative humidity exceeded 60% across much of northern Europe, while lower values were observed across southern and central Europe.

Mean relative humidity across Europe, 2017–2023.

Difference in mean relative humidity between the study period (2017–2023) and the reference period (2003–2016).

Relative humidity increased in parts of northern Europe, the Alps and Mediterranean coastal areas, while decreases were observed across parts of central Europe, Spain and Portugal.

Statistical analysis

Main finding

The analysis did not provide consistent statistical evidence of an association between changes in relative humidity and newly reported tick presence.

07

Limitations

Data quality

Tick observations were qualitative and did not provide information on abundance or detection effort.

Temporal uncertainty

The exact date of species arrival in many regions was unknown, limiting the reliability of the reference-period comparison.

Spatial scale

Aggregating observations to administrative regions introduces potential MAUP effects and may hide finer-scale ecological patterns.

Multiple drivers

Tick distribution is influenced by several interacting factors, including temperature, vegetation, soil moisture and host mobility.

08

Next steps

A stronger analysis would move beyond relative humidity alone and incorporate environmental and ecological drivers at a finer spatial resolution.

  • Use saturation deficit as a more biologically relevant humidity indicator.
  • Integrate temperature and winter conditions.
  • Incorporate vegetation and land-cover variables.
  • Use georeferenced tick observations rather than administrative presence/absence data where possible.
  • Model multiple environmental drivers jointly.
