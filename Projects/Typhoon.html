CLIMATE IMPACTS · VULNERABILITY

Typhoon Vulnerability & Exposure

Philippines Typhoon Rai · 2021

Validation of a geographic index combining population vulnerability and typhoon exposure using observed impacts and spatio-temporal meteorological data.

01

PROBLEM

How can population vulnerability and typhoon exposure be combined to assess disaster risk at the local scale?

The LEVERAge research project aims to develop a geographic index combining population vulnerability and exposure to typhoon hazards in the Philippines.

My contribution focused on validating the geographic index through two complementary approaches: observed loss and damage data from Typhoon Rai (2021), and the reconstruction of spatio-temporal meteorological conditions during the event.

02

DATA

From observed impacts to meteorological exposure

Observed impacts were obtained from the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC), including affected persons, affected families and damaged houses.

These observations were combined with 2020 Philippine census data and meteorological datasets describing precipitation, wind, atmospheric pressure and temperature during Typhoon Rai.

03

METHODOLOGY

Reconstructing typhoon exposure across municipalities

Observed loss and damage data were converted into relative shares of affected persons and families using municipality-level population and household data. This allowed impacts to be compared across municipalities with different population sizes.

Meteorological observations were processed from NetCDF datasets and harmonised spatially and temporally. The data were reprojected to a common geographic reference system, converted to local time and aggregated into eight-hour periods.

Precipitation from GPM/IMERG was aggregated as accumulated rainfall, while MERRA-2 wind, pressure and temperature data were summarised over the same eight-hour periods.

The resulting variables were normalised between 0 and 1 and combined to identify the time period corresponding to the highest meteorological exposure for each municipality.

04

RESULTS

What the data reveal

04.1

Observed loss and damage

The spatial distribution of affected populations broadly followed the trajectory of Typhoon Rai, while also revealing substantial variability between municipalities.

Relative shares of affected persons and families were calculated using 2020 census data. Higher proportions were generally observed in municipalities located closer to the typhoon's trajectory.

Relative share of affected persons across Philippine municipalities following Typhoon Rai.

04.2

Precipitation patterns

GPM/IMERG precipitation data captured the east-to-west progression of Typhoon Rai across the Philippines.

The strongest rainfall signals were detected during the passage of the typhoon, with the largest spatial extent of affected municipalities occurring during the 16 December period.

04.3

Meteorological exposure

Wind, pressure and temperature showed coherent spatio-temporal changes associated with the passage of the typhoon.

After harmonisation and normalisation, meteorological variables were combined to identify the eight-hour period of maximum exposure for each municipality. Exposure generally increased closer to the typhoon's trajectory.

05

KEY TAKEAWAYS

What this study shows

01

Observed impacts can support the validation of geographic vulnerability indicators. Loss and damage data provide an empirical reference for assessing how vulnerability indicators perform during a real disaster.

02

Meteorological exposure varies in space and time. Combining precipitation, wind, pressure and temperature makes it possible to characterise the changing exposure of municipalities during a typhoon.

03

Multi-source geospatial data can connect hazards with population impacts. Integrating meteorological observations, census data and observed damages provides a framework for analysing local differences in disaster exposure and vulnerability.
