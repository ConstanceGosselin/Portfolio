01

PROJECT OVERVIEW

Exploring a potential climate–vector relationship

Tick species are sensitive to environmental conditions that influence their activity, survival and geographic distribution. Changes in climate conditions may therefore contribute to changes in the areas where vector species are observed.

This project investigates whether changes in relative humidity between a historical reference period and 2017–2023 are associated with the appearance of five tick species in new European regions.

02

RESEARCH QUESTION

Can changes in relative humidity help explain the emergence of tick species?

The analysis focuses on five tick species occurring across different parts of Europe: Dermacentor reticulatus, Hyalomma marginatum, Ixodes persulcatus, Ixodes ricinus and Rhipicephalus sanguineus.

The central hypothesis was that changes in relative humidity could contribute to the appearance of species in regions where they had not previously been reported.

The study therefore combines a spatial comparison of climate and species distribution with a statistical analysis designed to test whether humidity change is associated with newly reported presence.

Importantly, the analysis is exploratory: tick distribution is influenced by multiple environmental and ecological drivers, so relative humidity cannot be interpreted in isolation.

03

DATA

Combining climate and species-distribution data

Relative humidity data from NASA's AIRS programme were downloaded as NetCDF files and processed in R. Daytime and nighttime observations were aggregated to obtain monthly and annual averages.

Tick distribution data were obtained from the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control and represented species presence across European administrative regions.

Climate rasters were spatially integrated with NUTS2 administrative regions, allowing the humidity signal to be compared with changes in reported species presence.

The climate analysis covered two periods: a 2003–2016 reference period and a 2017–2023 study period.

04

DATA PROCESSING

From NetCDF climate data to regional indicators

The downloaded NetCDF datasets did not contain an explicit coordinate reference system. The WGS84 geographic reference provided by the data source was therefore integrated before spatial processing.

Daytime and nighttime relative humidity values were averaged monthly, then aggregated to annual values. These annual datasets were subsequently aggregated over the study periods.

05

CLIMATE PATTERNS

Relative humidity varies substantially across Europe

Between 2017 and 2023, mean relative humidity exceeded approximately 60% across much of northern Europe, while values generally ranged between 45% and 60% across central and southern Europe.

The spatial pattern provides an environmental baseline for examining whether regions with newly reported tick presence experienced comparable changes in humidity.

Difference in mean relative humidity between the study period (2017–2023) and the reference period (2003–2016).

Relative humidity increased in parts of northern Europe, the Alps and Mediterranean coastal areas.

Decreases were observed across parts of central Europe, Spain and Portugal.

06

TICK DISTRIBUTION

Five species show different spatial patterns

The five species occupy distinct geographical ranges. Some are concentrated in northern Europe, others around the Mediterranean, while Dermacentor reticulatus and Rhipicephalus sanguineus show broader distributions.

The number of newly affected regions remained relatively limited for most species, although Hyalomma marginatum showed a substantially larger increase in reported presence.
