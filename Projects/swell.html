Coastal Storm-Swell Risk Modelling

Canary Islands North Atlantic storms

Analysis of the physical processes linking North Atlantic extra-tropical storms to swell generation, propagation and coastal impacts across the Canary Islands.

01

PROBLEM

How do North Atlantic storms generate the swells that impact the coasts of the Canary Islands?

Coastal areas of the Canary Islands can be affected by swells generated by extra-tropical storms developing over the North Atlantic. Understanding these impacts requires looking beyond storm intensity alone and considering the processes controlling swell generation, propagation and coastal interaction.

This project investigates the sequence linking storm dynamics, wind-generated fetch, swell propagation and coastal orientation in order to better understand how storm-generated wave systems reach different coastal locations.

02

RESEARCH QUESTIONS

From storm development to coastal impact

Where do the main North Atlantic storms affecting the Canary Islands develop?

How do storm characteristics and wind conditions control fetch generation and swell development?

How does the resulting swell propagate towards the Canary Islands?

Which processes determine the direction and spatial distribution of coastal impacts?

03

DATA

Connecting atmospheric and coastal processes

The analysis combines atmospheric, oceanographic and geographical information to reconstruct the pathway from storm development to coastal impact.

ERA5 reanalysis data were combined with storm-track information, fetch characteristics, wave and swell variables, and coastal geographical information.

04

METHODOLOGY

Following the storm–swell pathway

Conceptual framework linking storm development, fetch generation, swell propagation and coastal interaction.

01 — Storm direction

Storm trajectories were analysed to characterise the direction and dynamics of the extra-tropical systems responsible for the swell events.

02 — Fetch direction

Wind conditions were used to characterise the orientation of the fetch, representing the wave-generation area associated with the storm.

03 — Swell propagation

The resulting wave characteristics were analysed to investigate how swells propagate over long distances from their generation area towards the Canary Islands.

04 — Coastal impact direction

Finally, the incoming swell direction was compared with coastal characteristics to investigate how wave energy interacts with differently oriented coastlines.

05

SWELL DEVELOPMENT

From North Atlantic storms to Canary Island swells

Swells generated by North Atlantic storms can propagate over several thousand kilometres while maintaining a general direction. The analysis therefore follows the evolution of wave direction between the storm-generation area and the Canary Islands.

As the swell approaches the archipelago, geographical configuration becomes increasingly important. The orientation of individual coastal areas influences where incoming wave energy can be concentrated.
